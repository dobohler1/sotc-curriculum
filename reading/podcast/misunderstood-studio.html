On Air

MisunderstoodStudio

Allen Iverson wrote four hundred pages arguing that everybody got him wrong. Your job is to do that work yourself: take a story people think they know, and tell it so well that a stranger changes their mind. Then go do it for someone real.

Science on the Court · Reading Program · 8-week workshop · Grades 6–11 · Companion to Misunderstood: A Memoir (Iverson, 2025)

TRACK 01
THE JOB

What you are making

Read this part twice. Everything else is detail.

A five-episode podcast season plus a trailer, published on a public feed, under your name, with your cover art on it. Not a class recording. Not a project that gets turned in. A show that strangers can find and press play on.

Four episodes are about the book. The fifth is not — the fifth is you interviewing a real person in your life who has been misjudged, and telling their story the way Iverson told his. That episode is the whole point. The first four are how you get good enough to make it.

You do not pass by turning something in. You pass when twenty-five strangers understand you.

The gate

You cannot record an episode until every chapter it covers is passed at 90% in the reading exam app. The app is the gate, not the coach. No pass, no mic. That is the only prerequisite in this workshop, and there is no way around it.

TRACK 03
TEST2PASS

The pass bar

Five lamps. All five go green or you did not pass. There is no B.

KnowledgeAll 45 chapter exams passed at 90%+. Screenshot of your scoreboard.
ShippedTrailer + 5 episodes live on a public feed, with cover art and written descriptions, on the posted dates. Late is not shipped.
Clarity25 verified listeners outside SOTC. At least 20 of them can correctly answer “what was this episode about?”
Hot Seat8 live minutes in front of the room and an outside judge. No notes. Three binary calls.
The Real OneEpisode 5 carries 6+ minutes of original interview tape with a real person who is not in this workshop.

What the Hot Seat actually is

Eight minutes, live, at the launch party. The room is there. A working journalist, radio host, or podcaster is there, and they are the one who marks the card — not your coach. You bring nothing to the mic. They ask you about your own show. Three calls, each a yes or a no:

  1. Did you hold the room? Eight minutes, no collapse, no mumbling into your chest.
  2. Did you cite the book from memory? Two specifics you can pin to a chapter, out loud, unprompted.
  3. Did you take a hostile question? The judge will push on something you got wrong or soft. Answer it. Changing your mind out loud counts as passing; falling apart does not.

Clarity: how the listener check works

You hand out a card with a QR code. It opens a three-question form: What was this episode about? · Name one thing that happened in it. · Would you play another one? Yes / no. The third question is not scored. You are not being graded on being liked. You are being graded on being understood — the most common way to fail this lamp is a beautiful episode nobody could follow.

If a lamp stays red

You do not get a lower grade, because there are no grades. You write a one-page root cause: which lamp, why it failed, and the one thing you are changing. Then you retest in the next window. Iverson retook his whole life at City Farm. You can retake a lamp.

TRACK 04
AI RULES

AI is your producer, not your voice

Producers do prep, feedback, and cleanup. They never take the mic.

Green light

  • Drill me: “Ask me 20 questions on chapter 14 until I get all 20.”
  • Roleplay a hostile interviewer before the Hot Seat.
  • Read my draft back and tell me the exact sentence where a listener gets lost.
  • Transcribe my interview tape so I can find the good quote.
  • Clean noise and levels on audio I recorded.
  • Critique my cover art. Not make it.

Red light

  • AI writes a line you say out loud. Any line.
  • AI voices anything — a fake host, a fake Iverson, a fake you.
  • AI tells you what happened in the book. Every fact comes off a page you read.
  • AI writes your episode description or show notes.
  • AI writes interview questions you did not think of first.
How we catch it

The Hot Seat. If you cannot defend a sentence in your own episode, standing up, with no notes, then you did not write it. That is the entire detector, and it does not miss.

TRACK 07
BUILD SHEET

Episode build sheet

Same seven boxes every episode. Fill them before you touch a microphone.

  1. Gate check. Every chapter in this episode passed at 90%. Screenshot attached.
  2. The one question. Write the single question this episode answers, in one sentence. If it takes two, you have two episodes.
  3. Cold open. Fifteen seconds that make a stranger stay. Usually a moment, never a summary.
  4. Seven beats. Seven lines, five words each, in order. This is your whole script. There is no other script.
  5. Three quotes. Pulled from the book with chapter numbers. You say the chapter out loud when you use one.
  6. The turn. The place where the listener's first opinion stops being the obvious one. Every episode needs one.
  7. The ask. How you close, and what you ask the listener to do — scan the card, send it to one person.
TRACK 08
KIT

What a coach needs

Total cash outlay: a mic, if you want one. Everything else is free.

  • Recording. A phone in a quiet room beats a bad mic in a loud one. A closet full of coats is a professional-sounding booth — this is not a joke, it is what the soft clothes do to reflections.
  • Optional. One clip-on lav mic (~$25) shared across the cohort. Nice, not required.
  • Hosting. Spotify for Creators — free, records and publishes from a phone, generates a real public feed.
  • Editing. Audacity (desktop, free) or CapCut (phone, free).
  • Cover art. Canva, 3000×3000 px.
  • Listener check. One Google Form, three questions, one QR code, printed on cards.
  • The gate. The reading exam app the students are already using — the pass scoreboard on their own device is the record.
  • The judge. One outside adult who works with audio or words. Book them in week 1, not week 7.

Publishing rules, non-negotiable

  • First name and last initial only for anyone under 18. No schools named on tape, no schedules, no addresses.
  • Guest consent recorded on tape at the top of every interview: “Is it okay if I record this and put it in a podcast?” Their yes stays in the file.
  • Parent permission on file before any student’s voice goes on a public feed.
  • Coach listens end to end before anything publishes. No exceptions, including for The Answer tier.
  • The book has profanity. The show does not. Same rule the exams run on. You can quote a hard scene without quoting a hard word — doing that well is a skill, and it is part of the assignment.

TRACK 10
THE POINT

Why this assignment exists

A book report proves you read something to one adult who has to read it. A published episode proves you understood something to twenty-five people who owe you nothing. Those are not the same skill, and only one of them is worth eight weeks.

Iverson’s entire argument is that people decided who he was before they listened. Every student in this room has felt some version of that. The last episode is where that stops being a book about somebody famous and becomes a microphone pointed at somebody real — and the student holding it is the one who gets to decide the story is worth telling.

Anybody can be misunderstood. Not everybody can go make it stop.
