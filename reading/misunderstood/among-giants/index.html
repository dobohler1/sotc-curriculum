Misunderstood: A Memoir · Allen Iverson · Two days

Among Giants

The last part of this book is called “Among Giants.” Allen Iverson was six feet tall and about 165 pounds in a league full of men seven feet tall, and he went to the Finals anyway. That is why I gave you this book, and it is the reason this assignment exists.

You are going to go back through the six parts of the memoir and pull out the one most valuable thing in each — not what happened, but what a player can actually use. Then you're going to teach all six to people who never read it. Then you're going to do one of them, this week, for real.

Extract→ Teach→ Apply

What you're making

One podcast episode, 10 to 12 minutes, published for real.

Six lessons, one from each part of the book, in order. Each one gets about ninety seconds: the moment it came from, what it means, and what you're doing about it. That last part is what makes it your show instead of a book report — nobody has ever heard the version where a 12U point guard explains what he's changing on Friday because of chapter 24.

How you pass

Three things. All three, or it doesn't count.

1 · ExtractSix lessons, one per part, each pinned to a real scene and a chapter number. A lesson someone could use — not a summary of what happened.
2 · TeachEpisode published. Three people who never read the book listen, and afterward each can name three of your six lessons without help.
3 · ApplySix “I will” lines, and one of them is already done before you record. You tell the listener what happened when you did it.
The one that matters

Lamp 3. Anybody can read a book and say it was inspiring. The test is whether a single thing you do in the gym is different on Friday than it was on Monday, and whether you can say out loud what it was. Pick the lesson that makes you a little uncomfortable and do that one.

What a real apply line looks like

This is where most people get vague and it's the whole point of the assignment. Yours have to be countable.

Doesn't count

“I'm going to work harder and be more aggressive on defense.”

Counts

“Friday at NBN, before practice: 150 catch-and-shoot threes, makes written down. And the first three possessions of every scrimmage, I pick up their guard at full court — even if I'm tired, especially if I'm tired.”

The test for an apply line: could somebody else watch you and tell whether you did it? If not, rewrite it until they could.

The two days

About two and a half hours each. Check the boxes.

Day 1 · Extract~2h 30m
0:00 · 60 minMine the six parts

Go part by part. For each one, answer the question in the left column and write the scene it came from with its chapter number. Skim what you need to — you already read it.

Rule: it has to be a lesson somebody could use, not a thing that happened.

1:00 · 30 minWrite the six apply lines

One per lesson. Countable. Tied to a real day on your actual schedule — Monday, Friday, Saturday with your brother, wrestling.

1:30 · 30 minDo one. Today.

Pick the one that makes you least comfortable and go do it before you record anything. Write down what actually happened, including if it went badly. That goes in the episode.

2:00 · 15 minThe cold open

Fifteen seconds that make a stranger stay. Start with a moment from the book or a moment from your own gym. Never start with “in this episode I'm going to…”

2:15 · 15 minName it, make the art

Show name, episode title, cover art in Canva at 3000×3000. Get it done today so tomorrow is only the microphone.

Day 2 · Teach~2h 30m
0:00 · 15 minSound check

Record in a closet with clothes in it — not a joke, the clothes kill the echo. Test the phone at one inch, four inches, a foot, and pick one. Say your cold open five times until it stops sounding read.

0:15 · 50 minRecord the six

Ninety seconds each, from your notes, talking — not reading. Same shape every time: the moment, the lesson, what you're doing about it.

Say the chapter number out loud each time. That's how a listener knows you actually read it.

1:05 · 25 minCut your three worst moments

Find the three worst seconds, delete them, and stop. Editing forever is how a podcast never comes out.

1:30 · 20 minPublish

Spotify for Creators. Title, description, cover art. Hit publish. It's live and it stays live.

1:50 · 30 minThe proof

Three people who never read the book listen all the way through. Then, no help: each names three of your six lessons. Write down what they got.

Dinner · 10 minThe hot seat

Three questions from Dad at the table. No notes, no phone, no going back to look.

Where the lessons go after this

Six apply lines, one done. The other five have dates on them. That page goes on the wall and gets checked off — the episode is the promise, the wall is whether you kept it. If four of six are done by the end of the week, that's a season-changing week, and it started with a book.

AI rules

AI is your producer. Producers do prep and cleanup. They never touch the microphone.

Green light

  • “Quiz me on Part IV until I can explain it without looking.”
  • “Read my six lessons back — which one is vague?”
  • “Ask me hard questions about this episode like you're trying to catch me.”
  • Cleaning up noise or volume on audio you recorded.

Red light

  • AI writes a single line you say out loud.
  • AI tells you what happened in the book, or picks your six lessons.
  • AI writes your apply lines. Those are about your life; it doesn't have one.
  • Any AI voice, anywhere in the episode.
How I'll know

The hot seat. If you can't explain one of your own six lessons at the dinner table with no notes, you didn't come up with it. That's the whole detector and it doesn't miss.

What you need

  • A phone and a closet. That's the studio. A phone in a quiet closet beats a real microphone in a loud room, every time.
  • Spotify for Creators — free, records and publishes from the phone, gives you a real feed.
  • Canva — cover art, 3000×3000 pixels.
  • The book. Every lesson needs a scene and a chapter number, so it stays open next to you.
  • The chapter quizzes, if you want to re-check a part before you record:
    dobohler1.github.io/sotc-curriculum/reading/misunderstood/ms/

One more rule: the book has language in it. Your episode doesn't. You can tell a hard scene without saying a hard word — doing that well is a skill, and it's part of the job.
